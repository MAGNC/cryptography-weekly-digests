# Weekly cryptography research digest

**Coverage:** first public postings from August 4–10, 2026. Cross-posts and minor repository updates were excluded; significant revisions are labeled separately below.

## Executive summary

The week’s most consequential claim is a classical quasipolynomial-time distinguisher for the Goppa-code structure underlying Classic McEliece, although its concrete attacks remain impractical. A separate result substantially improves the asymptotic running time for exact SVP, while new lattice-policy encryption work strengthens adaptive security without relying on witness encryption. On the implementation side, formally verified NTT generation and a power-analysis study of LESS illustrate the continuing gap between mathematical security, performant code, and physical leakage. These are fresh papers or author claims and have not yet received broad independent validation.

## Most relevant papers

### 1. [Quasipolynomial Cryptanalysis of the McEliece Cryptosystem (or: PIR Meets McEliece)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1630)

**Ashrujit Ghoshal, Yuval Ishai, Aayush Jain, Nuozhou Sun · August 10 · Post-quantum cryptanalysis**

The authors give a classical \(n^{O(\log n)}\)-time algorithm that distinguishes Goppa–McEliece public keys from random matrices in the asymptotic Classic McEliece regime. It covers every Classic McEliece parameter set considered by NIST and also leads to a heuristic ciphertext-decryption attack.

**Why it matters:** This challenges the long-standing intuition that a McEliece public key adequately hides its underlying Goppa-code structure.

**Caveat:** The concrete estimates remain impractical, and the decryption extension is heuristic; this is not a practical break of Classic McEliece.

### 2. [Solving the Shortest Vector Problem in \(2^{0.6039n}\) Time via Mid-point Hessian](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1597)

**Minki Hhan · August 6 · Lattice foundations**

The paper claims randomized exact-SVP algorithms taking \(2^{0.6039n+o(n)}\) classical time or \(2^{0.5411n+o(n)}\) quantum time, with \(2^{0.5n+o(n)}\) space. The method estimates Hessians of the periodic Gaussian near candidate half-shortest vectors and recovers the vector through preprocessing-based bounded-distance decoding.

**Why it matters:** If verified, this markedly lowers the asymptotic time bound for exact SVP from the previous \(2^{n+o(n)}\) benchmark.

**Caveat:** It remains highly exponential and does not translate directly into an equivalent reduction in deployed lattice-scheme parameters.

### 3. [Distributed Monotone Policy Encryption with Stronger Security for DNFs and Threshold Policies from Lattices](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1609)

**Rishab Goyal, Saikumar Yadugiri · August 6 · Post-quantum public-key cryptography**

The authors present the first statically secure distributed policy-encryption constructions from falsifiable lattice assumptions, improving over earlier selectively secure schemes or constructions based on witness encryption. Ciphertexts are polylogarithmic in the number of users for DNF policies and remain sublinear for threshold policies.

**Why it matters:** Stronger adaptive policy and corruption choices are important for realistic silent-threshold and distributed-encryption deployments.

**Caveat:** The constructions rely on decomposed or succinct LWE and the random-oracle model; their practical costs are not yet established.

### 4. [Code Generation of Faster Formally Verified NTT with Plantard Reduction](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1624)

**Donnie Y. Xu, Rajeev Gore, Amin Sakzad, Ron Steinfeld, Raymond K. Zhao · August 6 · Verified post-quantum implementation**

A generator produces constant-time C and Jasmin NTT implementations for ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and FN-DSA, automatically placing modular reductions using static bound analysis. The authors report 1.5–1.8× forward and 1.7–2.5× inverse-NTT speedups over reference C, alongside an EasyCrypt correctness chain for ML-KEM.

**Why it matters:** Automating both optimization and proof generation could reduce error-prone manual tuning in production post-quantum code.

**Caveat:** The stated end-to-end formal equivalence currently centers on ML-KEM; portability and proof coverage for the other generated schemes need further evaluation.

### 5. [Game Hopping in Lean](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06261)

**Stefan Dziembowski, Grzegorz Fabiański, Daniele Micciancio, Rafał Stefański · August 6 · Formal cryptographic proofs**

HOPSCOTCH represents game-hopping arguments as explicit Lean 4 objects and proves a general soundness theorem that constructs reductions and concrete advantage bounds. Demonstrations include encrypt-then-MAC, ElGamal, GGM, and the transformation from one-time secrecy to public-key IND-CPA security.

**Why it matters:** A reusable framework connected to Lean’s broader mathematical library could make machine-checked cryptographic proofs easier to inspect and extend.

**Caveat:** The current examples are relatively foundational; proof effort and scalability on large, implementation-oriented protocols remain open.

### 6. [Power Side-Channel Leakage Distinguishers on LESSv2.0](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1601)

**Maciej Czuprynko, Rishub Nagpal, Tobias Schneider, Sujoy Sinha Roy · August 6 · Side channels / post-quantum signatures**

The paper reports that sparse-versus-dense column processing during LESS Gaussian elimination leaks even in constant-time implementations. Simulations estimate that 300–2,357 signatures can support correlation-based key recovery, and the authors report physical experiments examining unmasked and masked implementations.

**Why it matters:** It shows that constant-time control flow alone does not eliminate data-dependent physical leakage in a current NIST additional-signature candidate.

**Caveat:** Trace requirements and success depend on the device and implementation; the effectiveness and cost of proposed countermeasures need broader testing.

### 7. [Adaptive-Input-Secure Updatable Private Set Union](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1626)

**Seongbong Choi, Jiseung Kim, Hyung Tae Lee · August 9 · Privacy-preserving protocols**

The authors introduce a multi-epoch PSU protocol supporting additions and deletions when future inputs can depend on earlier outputs. At \(2^{20}\) elements and small updates, their single-threaded benchmark reports 1.41-second LAN epochs and 30.9–98.9× speedups over rerunning static PSU.

**Why it matters:** Adaptive inputs and incremental changes more closely reflect long-lived private databases than one-shot PSU models.

**Caveat:** Security is semi-honest, set and update cardinalities leak, and the benchmark covers a particular update regime.

### 8. [SoK: Cryptographic Key Recovery for Cryptoasset Custody and Financial Technologies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07104)

**Francisco Javier Becerra Sanchez, Antonio Ken Iannillo, Radu State · August 7 · Applied cryptography**

This systematization reduces a 118-paper discovery set to 77 analyzed systems and distinguishes recovery of secrets, shares, credentials, account control, assets, and forensic artifacts. The authors identify recurring gaps in trust placement, post-recovery lifecycle handling, abuse resistance, metadata privacy, and usable evidence.

**Why it matters:** Treating “recovery” as several distinct security operations gives wallet and custody designers a more useful threat-modeling framework.

**Caveat:** Evidence and deployment maturity vary considerably across the surveyed systems, and the taxonomy inherits the review’s inclusion and coding judgments.

## Other notable papers

- **Zero knowledge and signatures:** [AES-Based Grinding for MPC-in-the-Head Signatures](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1625) replaces Keccak-based grinding with a two-AES-call construction analyzed in ideal-cipher and random-oracle models.
- **Protocol verification:** [A Sound Translation from Tamarin to ProVerif](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06315) translates 562 of 566 tested lemma tasks and reports agreement on 246 of 247 comparable non-XOR tasks; this is the newly posted full version of a CCS 2026 paper.
- **Authenticated encryption:** [A Note on the Influence of a Zero Length Nonce on GCM and GMAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06061) shows hash-key recovery and forgery when zero-length nonces are permitted by the ISO/IEC specification; NIST GCM explicitly excludes such nonces.
- **Post-quantum deployment:** [Lattice-based Signature Schemes for Bitcoin](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1628) reviews Dilithium, Falcon, and Hawk with emphasis on transaction footprint, long-lived outputs, implementation constraints, and wallet derivation.
- **Material revisions:** [Formal Security Analysis of the Olvid Messenger](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1622) reports verified core properties but weaker-than-claimed modern security, including failure under eCK-style models; [Preprocessed Private Function Evaluation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1631) reports roughly 3 ms online lookup for a \(2^{24}\)-entry table. Both repositories label these as major revisions of CCS 2026 work.

## Watch next

- Independent analysis of whether the McEliece distinguisher extends beyond its current asymptotic regime or yields better concrete key-recovery methods.
- Proof scrutiny and cryptographic-estimator updates following the new exact-SVP bound.
- Whether NIST’s additional-signature process or LESS implementers respond with mandatory masking, blinding, or algorithmic changes.
- Expansion of formally generated NTT proofs to complete ML-DSA and FN-DSA implementations, not only shared arithmetic kernels.
