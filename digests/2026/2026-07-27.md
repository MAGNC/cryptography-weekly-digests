# Weekly cryptography research digest

**Coverage:** first public postings from July 21–27, 2026. Cross-posts were deduplicated; older papers receiving only repository updates were excluded from the ranking.

## Executive summary

Two themes dominate this week: post-quantum implementation risk and quantum-cryptographic foundations. Separate papers reduce estimated HQC security through improved decoding and demonstrate practical electromagnetic leakage from its reference implementation, reinforcing that standardized parameters and secure implementations must be evaluated together. Concurrent results on unclonable encryption claim efficient, information-theoretic constructions, while another paper identifies a definitional flaw in expected-time quantum zero-knowledge simulation. A practical study of hybrid X.509 validation also finds that accepting a hybrid certificate often does not mean its post-quantum evidence affected the decision.

## Most relevant results

### 1. [Multilevel Amortized Gaussian Elimination in Information-Set Decoding: Applications to HQC and PCG](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1498)

**Kévin Carrier, Valérian Hatey, Laura Luzzi, Jean-Pierre Tillich · July 22 · Post-quantum cryptanalysis**

The authors refine partial-pivot reuse in information-set decoding and introduce MAGE-Stern. Under their logic-gate cost model, they estimate roughly a three-bit improvement against HQC and place the Category I parameter set near 140-bit security—about three bits below its stated NIST target—while also improving attacks on pseudorandom correlation generators.

**Why it matters:** HQC is headed toward standardization, so even moderate corrections to concrete security estimates deserve parameter-level review.

**Caveat:** This is an analytical cost estimate, not a demonstrated full key recovery; conclusions depend on the chosen cost model and optimization assumptions.

### 2. [Exploiting Load/Store Leakage of Sparse Vectors for Key Recovery in HQC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19109)

**Gustavo Banegas, Benjamin Smith, Jad Zahreddine · July 21 · Side channels / post-quantum cryptography**

This paper reports electromagnetic leakage caused by compiler-generated register spilling in Cortex-M4 code for HQC’s sparse secret vectors. The authors demonstrate zero-versus-nonzero word classification with approximately 500 traces for the stronger channel and 5,000 for the weaker one, then turn the recovered positions into decoding hints.

**Why it matters:** It identifies a concrete implementation surface in a NIST-selected construction, not merely a theoretical distinguisher.

**Caveat:** The experiments target a particular reference implementation, compiler output, and microcontroller; portability to hardened implementations remains to be established.

### 3. [How to Define Expected Quantum Polynomial-Time Zero Knowledge Simulation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1500)

**Zhengnan Lai, Nicholas Spooner, Max Tromanhauser · July 22 · Zero knowledge / quantum foundations**

The authors argue that an existing coherent-runtime definition of expected quantum polynomial time can, because of freedom in selecting unitary dilations, encode arbitrary classical decision problems and therefore need not represent efficient computation. They propose a restricted definition, show that it retains zero knowledge for foundational constant-round protocols, and claim the first constant-round malicious-verifier ZK proof system for QMA under the repaired simulation notion.

**Why it matters:** A flaw in the efficiency definition could invalidate the interpretation of multiple post-quantum simulation results even if individual proofs remain formally correct under the old definition.

**Caveat:** The proposed repair and its claimed preservation results require independent proof scrutiny.

### 4. [Unconditional Unclonable Encryption](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1511)

**Prabhanjan Ananth, Amit Sahai · July 24 · Quantum cryptography**

The paper gives an information-theoretically secure, one-time private-key unclonable-encryption construction for one-bit messages. The authors claim efficient encryption and decryption with exponentially small unclonable-indistinguishability advantage and no computational assumption.

**Why it matters:** Removing computational assumptions from an efficient unclonable-encryption construction would sharpen the primitive’s theoretical foundations.

**Caveat:** The present result covers one-time encryption of a single bit; broader message and reuse settings require additional machinery.

### 5. [Efficient Unclonable Encryption from Pauli Eigenstates](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1509)

**Seyoon Ragavan · July 23 · Quantum cryptography**

This independent construction encrypts a bit as a random eigenstate of a secret Pauli operator, using linear-time classical work and single-qubit operations. The paper proves an exponentially small excess success probability for two receivers and derives a many-time construction for longer messages under a pseudorandom-state assumption.

**Why it matters:** The construction is unusually simple operationally and claims an essentially optimal security bound for its ciphertext size.

**Caveat:** The strongest unconditional statement is again one-time and one-bit; the many-time extension introduces a computational assumption. The manuscript also discloses substantial AI assistance in finding and drafting a proof, making independent verification especially valuable.

### 6. [Exponentially Fewer-Server PIR from Sparser S-Decoding Polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22033)

**Aparna Gupte, Seyoon Ragavan · July 24 · Private information retrieval**

The authors reduce the number of servers needed to obtain a known subpolynomial communication bound from exponential in the parameter to any fixed number \(s\), assuming a stated number-theoretic conjecture. They construct optimally sparse decoding polynomials and report unconditional verification for \(s\leq15\).

**Why it matters:** Server count is a major obstacle in information-theoretic PIR, so an exponential reduction changes the conceptual trade-off.

**Caveat:** The general theorem is conjectural, and the construction is primarily a complexity result rather than a deployable PIR system.

### 7. [Classical Acceptance Is Not Hybrid Authentication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20800)

**Taesung Kim, Boheung Chung, Keonwoo Kim, Yousung Kang · July 23 · Post-quantum migration**

Across eight X.509 validation stacks, nine modes, and six certificate schemes, the authors find that most stacks accepting separable hybrid certificates can do so solely through the classical path. They also demonstrate a lifecycle case where a revoked post-quantum credential does not prevent acceptance because it lies outside the verifier’s effective decision.

**Why it matters:** Deployments could report “hybrid” authentication without obtaining post-quantum protection, creating a silent downgrade during migration.

**Caveat:** This is a preprint submitted to *Computers & Security*; standards profiles and implementations may change in response.

### 8. [Updatable Private Set Union: Generic Construction with Efficient Instantiation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1497)

**Seongbong Choi, Hyung Tae Lee · July 22 · Privacy-preserving protocols**

The paper introduces two-party private set union with incremental updates, gives a generic construction from PSI and tagged PSU, and proves simulation security against semi-honest adversaries. Its implementation reports 14.1–45.4× speedups and 4.8–59.1× communication reductions over full recomputation in the tested large-set regime.

**Why it matters:** Incremental updates better match long-lived private datasets than repeatedly recomputing an entire union.

**Caveat:** Costs still depend on the original set size, and the security model does not cover malicious participants.

## Other notable papers by topic

- **Post-quantum cryptography:** [SM4th and uBlockith](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1506) explores VOLE-in-the-head signatures based on Chinese block ciphers; [ZKPoSP](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1508) proposes zero-knowledge proofs of wallet-seed provenance; [PIP-NTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18533) presents an NTT hardware accelerator.
- **FHE and verifiable computation:** [BioZKFHE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22065) combines BGV computation and proof checking for encrypted biometric search; [Sarus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19146) applies homomorphic encryption to multi-vendor perception fusion.
- **Protocols and distributed systems:** [Floor-IT](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1496) claims two-round good-case latency for information-theoretic BFT at optimal resilience; [Catching Many Traitors](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1523) develops lower bounds and constructions for tracing multiple colluders.
- **Applied cryptanalysis:** [BF²](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1499) reports a low-cost FPGA/CPU framework reaching \(1.6\times10^9\) NT hashes per second for multi-target password recovery.
- **Privacy and cryptocurrencies:** [The Consensus Number of Untraceable Cryptocurrencies](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1503) formalizes how two anonymity-ledger designs trade storage against synchronization and fairness.
