# Weekly cryptography research digest

**Coverage:** first public postings from July 28–August 3, 2026. Cross-posts were deduplicated; older publications and repository-only updates were excluded from the ranking.

## Executive summary

This week’s strongest work spans proof efficiency, post-quantum deployment, and confidential computation. New results claim substantially faster proving for batched hashes and lower-latency CKKS bootstrapping, while a threshold-Regev paper resolves a longstanding combination of efficiency and security requirements under standard MLWE. Measurement work suggests post-quantum TLS is concentrating around one hybrid configuration and incurs little observable Internet latency, but deployment is heavily mediated by infrastructure providers. Two papers on verifiable LLM inference also expose a useful tension: proofs can establish correct evaluation under committed weights without necessarily proving that the advertised computational effort occurred.

## Most relevant results

### 1. [Proving Threshold Regev PKE from Adaptive Hint-MLWE](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1585)

**Yisol Hwang, Shuichi Katsumata, Seonhong Min, Guilhem Niot, Yongsoo Song · August 3 · Post-quantum threshold cryptography**

The authors give what they claim is the first security proof for threshold Regev encryption simultaneously supporting a polynomial modulus, non-interactive decryption, and CCA compatibility. Their main technical tool is adaptive hint-MLWE, which they tightly reduce to standard MLWE.

**Why it matters:** Those three properties are central to making lattice-based threshold encryption practical and compatible with current threshold-cryptography efforts.

**Caveat:** This is a new theoretical preprint; the adaptive reduction and simulation-based security proof need independent review, and concrete protocol performance is not the primary result.

### 2. [Flock: Fast Proving for Batch Boolean Computations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27491)

**Benedikt Bünz, Ron D. Rothblum, William Wang · July 29 · SNARKs / zero knowledge**

Flock is a hash-based SNARK optimized for batches of repeated Boolean computations such as SHA-256, Keccak, BLAKE3, hash chains, and Merkle paths. The prototype reportedly proves 42,000 SHA-256 compressions per second on one M4 Max core—over 9× the authors’ Binius64 comparison—and exceeds 660,000 BLAKE3 compressions per second on ten cores.

**Why it matters:** Hash evaluation is a major cost center in proof systems, so improvements here can affect rollups, signatures, authenticated data structures, and proof-oriented applications generally.

**Caveat:** These are prototype benchmarks on selected workloads and hardware; verifier cost, proof size, memory, and comparisons under uniform security parameters all matter.

### 3. [Low-Latency Bootstrapping for CKKS Using Roots of Unity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27401)

**Jean-Sébastien Coron, Robin Koestler · July 29 · Fully homomorphic encryption**

The proposed SPRU method embeds modular addition into complex roots of unity instead of homomorphically approximating modular reduction. The authors report reduced multiplicative depth and as much as a 5× latency improvement in OpenFHE for ciphertexts containing relatively few slots.

**Why it matters:** Bootstrapping remains one of CKKS’s most expensive operations; reducing its depth and ring-dimension requirements could improve latency-sensitive encrypted computation.

**Caveat:** The largest reported gains concern low slot utilization, so performance for densely packed, throughput-oriented workloads may differ.

### 4. [Send and Pretend: Exploiting Transcript Consistency Issues in End-to-End Encrypted Group Chats](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27510)

**Gabriel K. Gegenhuber et al. · July 29 · Messaging protocols / applied cryptanalysis**

This USENIX Security paper reports that major encrypted messengers do not guarantee that every group member observes the same transcript. Under a malicious-member model, the authors demonstrate selective omission, reordering, altered presentation, inconsistent linked-device views, and poll manipulation without clear interface warnings.

**Why it matters:** Message confidentiality and authenticity do not automatically provide a shared, consistent group history—an important distinction for voting, moderation, and sensitive coordination.

**Caveat:** The attacker must already participate in the group, and exploitability varies by application behavior and user interface; proposed mitigations may also involve compatibility trade-offs.

### 5. [Mind the Gap: Policy vs Reality in Post-Quantum TLS Deployment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29005)

**Nimesha Wickramasinghe, Frank Li, Sanjay Jha, Arash Shaghaghi · July 31 · Post-quantum migration**

Using more than two billion handshakes across one million domains and eleven vantage points, the authors find PQ-TLS deployment converging on a single hybrid construction and being driven substantially by managed infrastructure. They report no meaningful Internet-latency increase, while legacy TLS configurations remain widely available alongside PQ modes.

**Why it matters:** This provides unusually broad empirical evidence about real migration rather than laboratory performance or policy plans.

**Caveat:** Domain sampling and network vantage points can miss enterprise, private, regional, or non-web deployments; provider concentration may also make headline adoption counts misleading.

### 6. [Hollow-LLM Attack: Computationally Trivial Weights in Zero-Knowledge Verification of LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28884)

**Chen Gong, Beijie Liu, Mengyuan Li · July 30 · Zero knowledge / verifiable AI**

The paper distinguishes proving correct inference under committed weights from proving that the advertised amount of computation was necessary. Its “ghost weights” preserve the declared architecture and satisfy the proof circuit while allegedly collapsing the effective workload to that of a smaller model.

**Why it matters:** A customer could receive a valid proof yet still be misled about model capacity or paid computational effort.

**Caveat:** The attack targets claims about effort and model scale, not the algebraic soundness of the underlying proof; practical impact depends on exactly what a service promises.

### 7. [MOSAIC: Masked Outsourcing of Secure AI Computations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29221)

**James Hsin-yu Chiang, Sheila Zingg, Kari Kostiainen, Srdjan Čapkun · July 31 · Confidential computation**

MOSAIC masks large matrix multiplications so a weak trusted client can outsource model-and-input-private AI computation to an untrusted accelerator. It accepts controlled numerical error, uses Hadamard rotations to manage accumulated noise, and bases security on decisional LWE and LPN; the authors report promising behavior on 70-billion-parameter transformer workloads.

**Why it matters:** It explores a middle ground between expensive generic cryptography and trusting an entire accelerator stack.

**Caveat:** Correctness is deliberately approximate, and the security/performance conclusions depend on the masking parameters, error model, workload, and client assumptions.

### 8. [OpenLLM: Modular and Scalable zkSNARKs for Verifiable LLM Inference](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1578)

**Yunbo Yang et al. · August 2 · zkSNARKs / verifiable computation**

OpenLLM decomposes inference into reusable operators with dedicated proofs for nonlinear and finite-field approximations, then composes them into non-interactive end-to-end verification. The authors claim smaller proofs, cheaper verification, and improved numerical fidelity relative to selected interactive and end-to-end systems.

**Why it matters:** Operator-level modularity may make proof engineering more reusable across changing model architectures.

**Caveat:** The available abstract does not provide enough normalized benchmark detail to validate the comparative claims; the Hollow-LLM result also shows that correct operator execution does not inherently prove model quality or effort.

## Other notable papers by topic

- **Post-quantum signatures and implementations:** [The Non-Intersecting Codewords Problem](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1573) proposes a new assumption for MPC-in-the-head signatures and reports a 2,934-byte Level-I signature; [Composing SHA-2 and SHA-3 Hardware](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1572) presents a crypto-agile hash architecture for PQC implementations.
- **MPC and secret sharing:** [Tensor Encodings for SIMD HSS](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1569) studies near-linear slot packing for lattice-based homomorphic secret sharing; [Partial Derandomization for Leakage-Resilient Shamir Sharing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28757) cuts evaluation-point randomness from \(nd\log p\) to \(d\log p\) in a restricted parameter regime.
- **Foundations:** [Distributed Point Functions and Function Secret Sharing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27696) is a new survey by Boyle, Gilboa, Ishai, and Scholl rather than a new construction.
- **Threshold protocols:** [Anchor-DKG](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1570) supports deterministic shares for participants recurring across multiple committees. It is labeled a **major revision/CCS 2026 version**, so it was not treated as a newly disclosed result.
- **Privacy:** [GoldenRetriever](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29019) studies non-interactive homomorphically encrypted retrieval for private RAG; [MESS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28999) targets private approximate semantic search.

## Watch next

- Independent benchmarking of Flock and SPRU under standardized security, memory, proof-size, and packing conditions.
- Whether the threshold-Regev proof leads quickly to concrete protocols or NIST threshold-cryptography submissions.
- Protocol or UI changes from encrypted-messaging vendors addressing transcript equivocation.
- Clearer service definitions for verifiable AI: proof of output correctness, model identity, model quality, and computational effort are distinct guarantees.
