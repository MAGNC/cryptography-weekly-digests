# Weekly cryptography research digest

**Coverage:** first public postings from August 11–17, 2026. Cross-posts and routine repository updates were removed; conference full versions and material revisions are labeled separately.

## Executive summary

This week’s strongest cluster combines proof-system efficiency with machine-checked cryptographic reasoning: Limber targets costly integer arithmetic in SNARKs, while another paper formalizes the delicate adaptive composition argument behind FHE noise flooding. New protocol work claims substantially smaller lattice-based blind signatures, maliciously secure dealer-free function secret sharing, and practical robust anonymous broadcast. Applied results address ciphertext side channels in encrypted memory and compiler-introduced leakage in masked implementations. No substantive public follow-up to last week’s new McEliece distinguisher or exact-SVP algorithm appeared during this window.

## Most relevant papers

### 1. [Limber: Low Overhead SNARKs for Integers from Any PCS](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1635)

**Jessica Chen, Lucas Xia, Wilson Nguyen, Benedikt Bünz · August 12 · SNARKs / zero knowledge**

Limber constructs SNARKs for integer relations from a general polynomial-commitment scheme, avoiding much of the overhead normally incurred when integer arithmetic is represented inside a finite-field circuit. The authors report proving RSA arithmetic more than 67× faster than prior circuit-based approaches.

**Why it matters:** Efficient non-native integer arithmetic is important for proving RSA, legacy signatures, big-integer computations, and interoperability with existing cryptographic systems.

**Caveat:** The reported comparison concerns selected RSA workloads; total prover memory, setup assumptions, proof size, and performance with different commitment schemes require normalized evaluation.

### 2. [Concurrently Secure Compact Blind Signatures from Module-SIS](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1690)

**Olivier Blazy, Lola-Baie Mallordy, Weiqiang Wen · August 15 · Post-quantum signatures**

The protocol avoids a general zero-knowledge proof by arranging its final response to form a short GPV-style signature directly. The authors obtain 4.7 KB blind signatures—down from at least 22 KB in the cited lattice-based state of the art—with concurrent security based on Module-SIS.

**Why it matters:** Compact post-quantum blind signatures could materially improve anonymous credentials, privacy-preserving payments, and token systems.

**Caveat:** Rejection sampling makes the protocol probabilistically multi-round; honest users need an expected 1.1 rounds, while the authors bound malicious users at 2.6 rounds.

### 3. [Verified Pythagorean Composition for Adaptive Cryptographic Games: Noise Flooding in Homomorphic Encryption](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1671)

**Yi Lee, Alexandru Cojocaru, Junyi Liu, Xiaodi Wu · August 15 · FHE / formal verification**

This paper machine-checks, in Rocq and SSProve, the adaptive security argument for noise flooding in approximate homomorphic encryption. Its new relational logic accumulates conditional KL-divergence costs before converting once to statistical distance, preserving the required square-root rather than linear loss across adaptive queries.

**Why it matters:** A superficially plausible ordinary hybrid proof gives parameters that are too weak; verifying the sharper composition argument reduces the risk of subtle errors in FHE security analyses.

**Caveat:** The result verifies an abstract reduction, not a complete deployed FHE implementation or its concrete parameter selection.

### 4. [Actively Secure Two-Party Function Secret Sharing with Dynamic Cross-Phase Verification](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1697)

**Yujie Xue, Lin Liu, Rongmao Chen, Yizhen Jiang, Yuchuan Luo, Bing Sun, Shaojing Fu · August 16 · MPC / function secret sharing**

VeriFSS provides dealer-free function secret sharing secure against one static malicious corruption, binding key generation, the represented function, and subsequent evaluations into one execution. The implementation reports 5.7 ms to generate and 3.3 ms to certify a 16-bit-domain DPF key, with roughly 5.4% key-size overhead over its semi-honest baseline.

**Why it matters:** Malicious security without an external dealer makes FSS more useful as preprocessing for private inference, lookup, and other two-party computations.

**Caveat:** The model permits a specified single-bit selective-failure leakage, and the benchmark and proof are new and unusually intricate.

### 5. [DumboMix: Robust Asynchronous Anonymous Broadcast Made Practical](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1699)

**Wei Tang, Hanwen Feng, Jiliang Li, Yuan Lu, Qiang Tang · August 16 · MPC / anonymity**

DumboMix solicits client messages through \(n\) servers and eventually reveals them in a uniformly random order despite unpredictable delays and up to \(n/3\) Byzantine servers. For 1,024 messages, the authors report multi-fold to roughly 66× speedups over several prior mixing approaches in four-to-31-server LAN and WAN tests.

**Why it matters:** Combining anonymity with guaranteed delivery under asynchronous faults is valuable for voting, reporting, auctions, and censorship-resistant messaging.

**Caveat:** The system still performs expected quadratic public-by-shared scalar work, and the evaluations are controlled experiments rather than adversarial Internet deployments.

### 6. [Counters and Nonces for Mitigating Ciphertext Side Channels](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1632)

**Moritz Peters, Jens Alich, Ashwin Jha, Gregor Leander, Yuval Yarom, Tim Güneysu · August 12 · Confidential computing**

The paper addresses attacks that infer VM secrets by observing deterministic changes in encrypted memory. It proposes storing freshness counters or random nonces in repurposed ECC-memory space and reports designs combining freshness, integrity, and memory tagging at approximately 2% performance overhead.

**Why it matters:** This tackles the deterministic-encryption root cause of ciphertext side channels in technologies such as AMD SEV, rather than attempting to patch individual leakage patterns.

**Caveat:** Repurposing ECC storage changes reliability and platform assumptions; security and overhead must be validated in actual memory controllers and threat models.

### 7. [Equivalence Between Average-Case Hardness of Learning and Cryptography for Mixed Quantum States](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1691)

**Alexandru Cojocaru, Laura Lewis · August 15 · Quantum foundations**

The authors prove that average-case hardness of learning mixed quantum states is equivalent to the existence of inefficiently verifiable one-way state generators. Through existing connections, this also relates mixed-state learning hardness to EFI pairs and yields an oracle separation between two notions of one-way state generation.

**Why it matters:** It sharpens the boundary between quantum learning hardness and the minimal assumptions needed for quantum cryptographic primitives.

**Caveat:** This is a foundational equivalence rather than a construction with near-term parameters, and the oracle separation does not imply an unconditional separation in the standard model.

### 8. [Toward Secure Compilation: Leakage Detection for Masked Implementations in Jasmin](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1696)

**Nicolai Schmitt, Sven Wroblewski, Fabio Campos, Andreas Heinemann · August 16 · Side channels / verified compilation**

The authors add a compiler pass that tracks contacts among shares, secrets, randomness, and public values before register and stack allocation. It identifies potential masking-order reductions and explains their causes under a configurable microarchitecture-oriented leakage model.

**Why it matters:** Correct source-level masking can be undermined during compilation, so analysis inside a verified cryptographic compiler addresses an important implementation gap.

**Caveat:** Validation currently uses 60 targeted snippets; the pass detects rather than repairs leakage and remains dependent on the accuracy of its hardware model.

## Other notable papers by topic

- **Post-quantum implementations:** [Qlapoti+ and More](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1640) reports 1.2–2.2× end-to-end improvements for selected SQIsign and PRISM operations; [MAYO Lite](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1634) targets low-RAM Cortex-M4 implementations.
- **Post-quantum foundations:** [Relations Between the Uniform MQ Assumption and Other Multivariate Assumptions](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1694) establishes tight or parameter-dependent implications among UMQ, MQ one-wayness, and second-preimage resistance.
- **Applied cryptanalysis:** [Multi-Diagonal Truncated Differentials and Ciphertext-Only Attacks on Reduced-Round AES](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1637) systematizes a family of reduced-round attacks; [Rotational-Quasidifferential Framework](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1633) gives a geometric treatment of rotational-XOR cryptanalysis.
- **Data structures:** [Pruning Merkle-Tree Consistent Accumulator](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1685) supports prefix pruning and proof updates while preserving conventional Merkle-style verification.
- **AI-assisted mathematics:** [Theoretical Open Problems in Symmetric Cryptography](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1687) claims formally checked resolutions or advances across 14 Boolean-function problems, including the pointwise Tu–Deng conjecture. The scale and novelty of these claims warrant careful independent inspection of both statements and proof artifacts.
- **Conference versions and revisions:** [LUNA+](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1639), a CCS 2026 paper, reports smaller lattice-based post-quantum SNARK proofs and CRS data. [Fair-Weather No More](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1677) and [How to Use Polynomially-Hard iO](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1636) are explicitly labeled major revisions of CRYPTO 2026 publications and were therefore not ranked as new disclosures.
